# Conviction Enhanced: Geopolitical Strategy Game Rules

## Overview

Conviction Enhanced models the strategic competition between major power blocs (USA, EU, China) through targeted policy actions, economic leverage, and technological advancement. The key innovation is that **policy cards must target specific opponents**, creating more realistic diplomatic dynamics.

## Core Mechanics

### 1. Targeting System

Every policy card now includes:
- **Target Selection**: Choose to hit one specific opponent OR both opponents
- **Cost Scaling**: Targeting "Both" costs 2× the base GDP cost
- **Effect Distribution**: Multi-target attacks apply ½ effect (rounded up) to each target

Example:
- USA plays Financial Sanctions → China: Costs 5 GDP, China loses 3 GDP
- USA plays Financial Sanctions → Both: Costs 10 GDP, EU and China each lose 2 GDP

### 2. Signature Domains & Lever Tracks

Each bloc has a historical strength that provides cost discounts:

| Bloc | Signature Domain | Real-World Basis |
|------|-----------------|------------------|
| USA | Finance | Dollar dominance, SWIFT control, capital markets |
| EU | Regulation | GDPR, environmental standards, consumer protection |
| China | Trade | Manufacturing hub, trade surplus, supply chains |

**Lever Tracks (0-3)**:
- Each point reduces costs by 1 GDP for cards in your signature domain
- Example: USA with Finance Lever 2 pays only 3 GDP for Financial Sanctions (base 5)

### 3. Turn Sequence

1. **Back Channel**: Negotiate alliances and deals privately
2. **Policy Draft**: 
   - Generate GDP income
   - Allocate budget (military, tech, culture, infrastructure, diplomacy)
   - Select policy card and target(s)
3. **Resolution**: 
   - Apply budget effects
   - Resolve card battles (counters still apply)
   - Apply targeted effects
4. **Headlines**: Random events affect blocs

## New Policy Cards

### Financial Warfare
- **Financial Sanctions**: Freeze GDP and damage economic development
- **Secondary Sanctions**: Force others to join sanctions or suffer penalties
- **Debt Trap**: Convert loans into territorial influence

### Trade & Industry
- **Dumping Products**: Destroy opponent's industrial base
- **Anti-Dumping**: Defensive card that protects and boosts economy
- **Rare Earth Embargo**: Cripple tech and military production

### Technology Control
- **Tech Transfer**: Share technology with allies (both gain)
- **Export Controls**: Block opponent's tech advancement
- **Cyber Operations**: Enhanced cyber espionage options

### Regulatory Power
- **Regulatory Clampdown**: Massively increase opponent's bureaucratic drag
- **Carbon Tariff**: Tax opponents unless they have green infrastructure
- **Standards Push**: Force compliance costs on rivals

## Victory Conditions

First to **50 Victory Points** wins. Points come from:
- GDP tokens (1 VP each)
- Tech levels (1/2/5 VP cumulative)
- Military posture (2 VP per level)
- Cultural influence (1 VP per point)
- Controlled territories (varies)
- Alliance network (2 VP per alliance)

## Strategic Considerations

### When to Multi-Target
- **Pros**: Hit both opponents, prevent ganging up
- **Cons**: Expensive, reduced effectiveness, can unite opponents

### Alliance Dynamics
- Tech Transfer only works on allies
- Secondary Sanctions can break up opposing alliances
- Trust reduces effective regulatory drag

### Domain Specialization
- Focus budget on raising your lever track
- Use signature cards when possible for maximum efficiency
- Counter opponent's signature moves to negate their advantage

## Real-World Parallels

The game models actual geopolitical tactics:

1. **US Financial Hegemony**: Dollar weaponization, SWIFT exclusion
2. **EU Regulatory Power**: GDPR fines, carbon border adjustment
3. **Chinese Trade Leverage**: Rare earth export controls, market access
4. **Secondary Sanctions**: Iran sanctions affecting European banks
5. **Tech Decoupling**: Chip export controls, Huawei bans
6. **Dumping**: Chinese steel overcapacity, solar panel price wars

## Quick Start Guide

1. **Turn 1**: Build your lever (spend on signature domain infrastructure)
2. **Turn 2-3**: Use discounted signature cards against key rival
3. **Mid-game**: Form alliances for tech transfer and VP bonuses
4. **Late-game**: Multi-target to prevent opponents from reaching 50 VP

## Card Counter Relationships

The rock-paper-scissors system remains:
- Financial Sanctions ← countered by → Trade Deal
- Regulatory Clampdown ← countered by → Lobbying Blitz
- Dumping Products ← countered by → Anti-Dumping
- Tech Transfer ← countered by → Export Controls

Remember: Counters only work if the counter-card targets the attacker!

## Tips for New Players

1. **GDP Management**: Don't overspend on multi-targeting early
2. **Lever Investment**: Prioritize your signature domain infrastructure
3. **Target Selection**: Hit the leader, but watch for counter-alliances
4. **Defensive Play**: Keep GDP for counter-cards against likely attacks
5. **Alliance Timing**: Form alliances before tech transfer opportunities

## Advanced Strategies

### The Sanctions Cascade
Use Secondary Sanctions to force neutrals to join your Financial Sanctions, creating a GDP death spiral for your target.

### The Green Trap
Invest heavily in infrastructure, then play Carbon Tariff to tax both opponents while you're immune.

### The Tech Alliance
Form an alliance, use Tech Transfer to boost both parties, then break the alliance once you've pulled ahead.

### The Regulatory Fortress
As EU, max out Regulation lever and spam Regulatory Clampdown at reduced cost while building cultural influence.
